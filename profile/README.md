## ARTA Highlights

- **Two-Channel Transfer:** ARTA audio analyzer can compare stimulus and response through an interface you already use, which is the lab pattern rather than a single-mic RTA walk.
- **Impulse And Frequency:** ARTA impulse response captures how a driver or a room-under-test you control rings, then FFT turns that into a curve.
- **THD On A Stepped Sweep:** ARTA distortion test (STEPS) is for harmonic plots on a loudspeaker or electronics you already have on the bench.
- **Impedance With LIMP:** ARTA lab measurement family includes impedance work on a driver you own, useful before a crossover is cut.
- **Generator Plus Scope Habit:** Signal generator, spectrum, and recorder live together so a prototype box you built does not need three separate meters.

## What Is ARTA

ARTA is a Windows measurement suite aimed at people who already build or repair loudspeakers and audio electronics. Where Room EQ Wizard often lives in a finished listening room, ARTA audio analyzer is at home on a bench: a mic on a stand, a DUT on the jig, a two-channel interface. ARTA impulse response is the gated snapshot. ARTA distortion test is the stepped sine that shows whether a driver you own is clean at the level you asked. ARTA lab measurement is the overall habit - plots you save with the prototype.

Installers sometimes use it in rooms, but the culture of the program is laboratory: calibrated chain, known stimulus, named traces. LIMP impedance and STEPS distortion are why crossover designers keep it around. It is not a live-sound dual-FFT platform like Smaart, and it is not a music editor.

Work on drivers, boxes, and electronics you built, own, or were hired to measure. Keep calibration files with the interface you administer. Do not treat a forum screenshot as a substitute for a trace from your bench.

## Starting with ARTA

Prerequisites: A PC you administer, a two-channel interface, a measurement mic, and a speaker or circuit you are allowed to test.

1. **Prepare:** Wire stimulus and return so ARTA audio analyzer sees a loop you understand, then set safe levels.
2. **Configure:** Load mic calibration, choose sample rate, and name the project after the driver or box on the bench.
3. **Test:** Capture a short ARTA impulse response and confirm the peak is not cable buzz.
4. **Refine:** Run ARTA distortion test at a level the driver you own can actually take.
5. **Apply:** Save traces with the prototype notes, and repeat after each crossover change.
6. **Maintain:** Recheck the loopback of the interface you administer whenever cables change.
